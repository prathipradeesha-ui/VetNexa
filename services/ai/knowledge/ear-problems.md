## Summary

Ear infections are common, especially in dogs with floppy or hairy ears and in pets with allergies. Kittens and puppies often have ear mites. Ear problems are painful and can become long-lasting if not treated properly.

## What you can do

- Look for redness, discharge, a bad smell or swelling.
- Do not push cotton buds into the ear canal.
- Do not put drops or oils in the ear before a vet has checked it. Some drops can cause harm if the eardrum is damaged.
- Use only an ear cleaner your vet recommends, as directed.

## See a vet if

- Your pet shakes its head, scratches its ears, or has a smelly, red or swollen ear.
- The ear flap suddenly swells like a cushion (an aural haematoma).

## Get help now if

- Your pet has a sudden head tilt, loss of balance, walks in circles, or its eyes flick from side to side.
