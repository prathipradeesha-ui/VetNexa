## Summary

Core vaccines protect every cat against feline panleukopenia (feline enteritis) and the two main "cat flu" viruses, feline herpesvirus and calicivirus. Where rabies is present, including Sri Lanka, rabies vaccination is also recommended. Indoor cats still need core vaccines because viruses can be carried in on shoes and clothing.

## Typical schedule

- Kittens usually start at 6–8 weeks of age, with doses every 2–4 weeks until about 16 weeks old or older.
- A booster is given between 6 months and 1 year of age.
- Adult boosters follow your vet's plan, based on your cat's lifestyle and local disease risk.

## What you can do

- Keep a new kitten indoors and away from unknown cats until its course is complete.
- Record each vaccination in VetNexa so you are reminded when the next one is due.

## See a vet if

- Your cat has swelling, vomiting or breathing difficulty after a vaccine.
- A lump at an injection site is still there three months later, or keeps growing.
