## Summary

Fleas cause itching and skin disease and spread tapeworms. Ticks spread serious infections such as tick fever (ehrlichiosis and babesiosis), which are common in warm climates like Sri Lanka. Year-round prevention is the best protection.

## What you can do

- Use a flea and tick product your vet recommends, every month or as the label says.
- Never use a dog product on a cat. Many dog spot-ons contain permethrin, which can kill cats.
- Check your dog for ticks after walks, especially the ears, neck, between the toes and under the legs.
- Remove a tick with a tick hook or fine tweezers: grip close to the skin and pull or twist steadily. Do not burn it or cover it with oil.
- Wash bedding and vacuum often, and treat every pet in the home at the same time.

## See a vet if

- Your pet becomes tired, stops eating, or has pale gums, a nosebleed or dark urine after tick exposure. These can be signs of tick fever.
- The skin is broken, infected or losing hair.
