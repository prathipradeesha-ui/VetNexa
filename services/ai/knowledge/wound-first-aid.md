## Summary

Small grazes can often be cared for at home, but cuts, bites and punctures frequently need a veterinarian. Bite wounds may look tiny on the surface while hiding deep damage and infection underneath, and cat bites very often turn into abscesses.

## What you can do

- If it is bleeding, press a clean cloth firmly on the wound for several minutes.
- Rinse gently with clean water or saline. Avoid hydrogen peroxide, alcohol and human creams.
- Stop your pet licking it (a cone or a clean T-shirt can help).
- You can use VetNexa Wound check for a cautious first look and advice on how urgent it is.

## See a vet if

- The wound is deep, gaping, longer than about 1 cm, or was caused by a bite.
- It becomes swollen, hot, smelly or oozes pus, or your pet is in pain or unwell.

## Get help now if

- Bleeding does not stop after 5–10 minutes of firm pressure, or blood is spurting.
- The wound is on the chest, abdomen or eye, or something is stuck in it (do not pull it out).
