## Summary

Gastric dilatation-volvulus (GDV, or "bloat") happens when a dog's stomach fills with gas and can twist. Blood supply to the stomach and other organs is cut off. It is most common in large, deep-chested breeds such as German Shepherds, Great Danes and Rottweilers, and it can be fatal within hours without surgery.

## Warning signs

- A swollen, tight belly.
- Repeated retching or trying to vomit with little or nothing coming up.
- Restlessness, drooling, pacing or looking at the belly.
- Weakness, pale gums or collapse.

## Get help now if

- You notice these signs. Go to a veterinarian immediately; do not wait to see if it improves.

## Prevention

- Feed large breeds two or more smaller meals a day instead of one large meal.
- Avoid hard exercise for an hour before and after meals.
- Ask your vet about preventive surgery (gastropexy) for high-risk breeds.
