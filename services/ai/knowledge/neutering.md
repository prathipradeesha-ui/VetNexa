## Summary

Neutering (castration of males and spaying of females) prevents unwanted litters, reduces the number of stray animals and lowers the risk of some diseases, such as womb infections and some cancers. In cats it also reduces roaming, fighting and spraying. The best age varies by species, breed and size.

## What you can do

- Discuss timing with your vet. Cats are often neutered at around 4 months of age; for dogs the best age depends on breed and size.
- Keep females away from entire males while they are in season.
- Ask about local low-cost neutering programmes for stray and community animals.

## After surgery

- Keep your pet calm and stop it licking the wound for 10–14 days.
- Check the wound daily, and contact your vet if it is red, swollen, open or oozing.
