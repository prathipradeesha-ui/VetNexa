## Summary

Pets cannot cool down by sweating. In hot weather, in a parked car or after exercise, their body temperature can rise to dangerous levels within minutes. Flat-faced breeds such as pugs, bulldogs and Persian cats, overweight pets and older pets are at highest risk.

## Warning signs

- Heavy panting, drooling, and bright red or dark gums.
- Weakness, wobbling, vomiting or diarrhoea.
- Confusion, collapse or seizures.

## Get help now if

- Your pet shows any of these signs after heat or exercise. Start cooling it and go to a veterinarian straight away.

## What you can do now

- Move your pet to shade or an air-conditioned place.
- Pour cool (not ice-cold) water over its body and use a fan. Let it drink small amounts of water.
- Go to a veterinarian straight away, even if it seems to recover, because organs can still be damaged.

## Prevention

- Never leave a pet in a car, even with the windows open.
- Walk early in the morning or late in the evening, and always provide shade and water.
